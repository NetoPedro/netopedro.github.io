About

Pedro C. Neto (Pedro Carneiro Neto) is a Portuguese artificial intelligence scientist. At Unilabs he builds AI models for the medical ecosystem, from data and training through regulatory compliance and privacy, and he is an Invited Assistant Professor at the Faculty of Engineering of the University of Porto (FEUP), where he teaches machine learning.

He holds a PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering from FEUP, with a thesis on explainability, fairness and privacy in AI for biometrics, an MSc in Computer Science from Aalto University in Finland, and a BSc in Informatics Engineering from ISEP. Before Unilabs, he spent four years as a researcher at INESC TEC, where he co-developed an AI system for colorectal cancer diagnosis from whole-slide images that was covered by national and international press.

In 2024 the European Association for Biometrics gave him the Max Snijder Award for his work on interpretable face-matching confidence.

Medical AI in practice

At Unilabs I work where most medical AI projects stall: turning a model that works on a benchmark into one that can be used safely in a regulated diagnostic network. The specific products are confidential, but the work spans three areas.

Models at population scale

Training and validating models on real data, across imaging, pathology and other clinical data. Generative AI with strong guardrails on non-clinical domains.

Regulation built in

Developing models with the evidence the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR) and In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation (IVDR) require, from design documentation to performance evaluation.

Privacy by design

Data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) and GDPR-compliant data pipelines for sensitive health data. Self-Hosted Solutions for Large-Language Models deployment and integration on workflows.

Research

Medical AI and computational pathology

Diagnostic models for whole-slide images that work with the annotations pathologists can realistically provide.

  • An interpretable machine learning system for colorectal cancer diagnosis from pathology slides
    npj Precision Oncology, 2024

    Semi-supervised multiple-instance learning that combines weak and full annotations. Released with a dataset of more than 5,000 exams (about 7 TB).

  • Annotating for artificial intelligence applications in digital pathology: a practical guide for pathologists and researchers
    Modern Pathology, 2023
  • CAD systems for colorectal cancer from WSI are still not ready for clinical acceptance
    Scientific Reports, 2021
  • QLabelMIL: inter-pathology query decoding for multi-label gastric histopathology
    MICCAI Workshop, 2026

Trustworthy biometrics

Face recognition that can explain its decisions, treats demographic groups fairly and can be trained without harvesting personal data.

  • PIC-Score: probabilistic interpretable comparison score for optimal matching confidence in single- and multi-biometric face recognition
    CVPR Workshops, 2023 EAB Max Snijder Award
  • Model compression techniques in biometrics applications: a survey
    Information Fusion, 2025
  • Compressed models decompress race biases: what quantized models forget for fair face recognition
    BIOSIG, 2023
  • Causality-inspired taxonomy for explainable artificial intelligence
    arXiv, 2022

More than 25 peer-reviewed papers, cited 906 times with an h-index of 18 on Google Scholar (October 2026). PhD thesis: Explainability, Fairness and Privacy in AI for Biometrics, University of Porto, 2025.
